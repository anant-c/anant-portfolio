Why Indian Startups Are Unprofitable | KATA 6

Varun Mayya · 0:38:43 · Watch on YouTube
Attribution is fundamentally misunderstood; relying on easily measurable metrics like ROAS ruins brands, while the real drivers of success are untrackable, long-term, and qualitative.

TL;DR

  • Relying on highly measurable metrics like ROAS leads to a performance marketing death spiral, continuously driving up unit costs and killing brand aspiration.
  • Customers don't buy instantly after seeing an ad; their purchasing journey spans weeks of research, reviews, and qualitative decisions that attribution forms fail to capture.
  • "How did you hear about us" surveys give garbage data because busy people provide low-context, random answers just to get past them.
  • In life and business, people incorrectly attribute success to singular factors (like degrees or sheer hard work), when success actually stems from a multifactorial cluster of elements.

What Is Attribution?

00:59 Attribution means figuring out the exact thing responsible for your success or failure. Mayya opens with a striking example: in a room of 300 people, roughly 30 crores (10 lakhs per person) were spent on degrees, yet nobody believes their degree directly led to their current financial success. This misattribution—believing X caused Y when it actually didn't—is a pervasive problem in both marketing and life. When people or companies succeed without knowing why, they cannot repeat it; it becomes random luck. Conversely, in team projects like a successful YouTube channel, everyone from the editor to the motion graphics artist will claim they were the crucial differentiator. Knowing how to correctly attribute success allows you to promote the right talent and replicate winning formulas.

ROAS Is Killing Your Brand

05:06 The worst brands in the world scale using ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)—spending 10 rupees to make 20. This creates a moatless business. Over time, the cost of acquiring new audiences increases because you exhaust the people who are easily convinced. The ad that cost 10 rupees today will cost 15 tomorrow, and eventually 22. To maintain margins, you are forced to raise the price of your product, becoming an objectively worse business. The best brands (like Dyson, Apple, or a16z) don't sell on ROAS; you cannot pinpoint the exact billboard or YouTube video that converted you into a fan. Marketing managers, however, prefer ROAS because it is easy to measure and justify to their bosses, even if it harms the company in the long run.

How Nike Lost $25 Billion

07:50 Nike provides a cautionary tale of chasing data. Historically, Nike built aspiration by celebrating sports and athletes. However, in 2020, they shifted from brand marketing to digital performance marketing, prioritizing sales activation over demand creation. They used tracking pixels to heavily retarget users who had already shown intent (like abandoning a cart), treating it as a highly measurable strategy. But as they focused strictly on unit sales, Nike lost its story and connection with audiences. The brand became less aspirational, and this shift cost them heavily in market valuation.

The Performance Marketing Death Spiral

09:52 In India, the reliance on performance marketing is a primary reason why startups remain unprofitable. As ad costs climb, companies hit a ceiling where customers refuse to pay higher unit prices, leaving the business structurally unprofitable. Some companies initially build massive organic distribution (like Physics Wallah or Zerodha), but after raising funding, they pivot to "professional" celebrity ads and performance marketing, forgetting their roots. Zerodha is the counter-example: they have practically zero cost of acquisition because they built organic reach, meaning their entire profit margin is preserved without being handed over to advertising platforms.

Your Survey Data Is Lying to You

13:20 Marketers often rely on "How did you hear about us?" drop-down menus to track attribution. Mayya points out this data is completely flawed. Users are interacting with these forms in low-context, distracted states—often on their phones, multitasking in a metro or on the toilet. They select the first option just to move on. If you assume this garbage data is the truth, you will wrongly attribute sales to television ads or random channels. Furthermore, affiliate and referral links are equally misleading. If a buyer watches five different personal finance videos about Zerodha but only clicks the link on the final one, it is inaccurate to credit only the last creator for the conversion.

The Proxy Attribution Hack

16:50 Because exact attribution is nearly impossible, smart companies use "proxy attribution." Netflix evaluates a new movie not just by direct viewership, but by its ability to drive new subscriptions to the catalog. Sony makes console-exclusive games like Spider-Man because they know a strong library pulls players into the PlayStation ecosystem. Mayya shares his own proxy metric for Instagram: shares. When promoting events like Mumbai Tech Week or Nvidia's GTC, views and likes are vanity metrics. People tap "like" without thinking. But a share—sending the reel in a DM saying "let's go to this"—is a direct measure of intent.

How People Actually Buy Things

20:48 Marketers often build campaigns around coupon codes ("Use VARUN1000"), assuming users will see a link and snap-buy a product. In reality, purchasing is a long, qualitative journey. A buyer might see a celebrity endorse a new phone, which sparks interest. But instead of buying immediately, they spend weeks watching YouTube reviews (like MKBHD), comparing specs with three competitors. They wait 30 days for their next paycheck. By the time they have the money, they evaluate a fourth competitor. Ultimately, the final decision is a gut choice based on status and satisfaction, not just specs.

Bad Products Advertise for Competitors

24:00 Because of the long buyer's journey, running ads for a weak product is dangerous. If your product is subpar, your marketing simply triggers the user's research phase. They will see your ad, go online to compare alternatives, discover your competitors have better offerings, and buy from them instead. Weak competitors advertising against stronger ones are effectively subsidizing the stronger brand's customer acquisition.

The Only Attribution Method That Works

25:08 The only reliable way to attribute sales is qualitative, manual work. You must get on the phone with your buyers, talk to them, and ask them exactly why they purchased (or didn't). This requires an honest, unbiased team member who is willing to listen rather than push a narrative. Unless you are at a massive scale where big data reveals macro trends, direct customer conversations are the only way to figure out what is actually working.

Did Your Degree Even Matter?

26:36 Attribution applies heavily to life choices. Many believe their degree got them their job, when the risk-reward ratio (spending 10 lakhs for zero direct correlation to wealth) says otherwise. People wrongly attribute their success to random skills (like learning motion graphics), fad diets, or sheer hard work. If hard work alone caused success, manual laborers would be billionaires. Hard work is a prerequisite, but not the differentiator. If you wrongly attribute your success, you will waste time pulling the wrong levers in the future.

The Actual Formula for Business Success

36:00 Instead of getting distracted by sensational business case studies or believing that capital scales any business, Mayya boils down true business success to three core factors: finding an untapped market, hiring good talent and executing well, and sticking around long enough to reap the benefits. Most tricks and growth hacks can be easily copied by competitors. Success comes from identifying the right multifactorial forces—what Charlie Munger calls the Lollapalooza effect—and letting them compound over time.

Do This

  • Call your recent buyers directly to ask why they purchased instead of relying on drop-down forms.
  • Track proxy metrics like "shares" instead of vanity metrics like views or likes to measure true intent.
  • Build long-term organic reach (like a YouTube channel) to lower your customer acquisition costs to zero.
  • Stop optimizing purely for ROAS if you are scaling, and invest in unmeasurable brand building.
  • Evaluate your life choices to find out what actually drives your success, rather than blindly assuming it's your degree or hard work.

Glossary

Attribution
The act of identifying the exact factor responsible for your success or failure.
ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)
A metric calculating revenue generated for every dollar spent on advertising, often misleading in the long term.
Proxy Attribution
Using indirect, correlated metrics (like DM shares or library subscriptions) to measure intent when direct attribution is impossible.
Lollapalooza effect
A concept by Charlie Munger describing multiple forces acting in the same direction to produce a massive outcome.
